# Messaging and voice

**Primary idea:** Catalyst of analysis. **Source:** explicit user direction. The existing homepage's warm verbal foundation is user-valued; new supporting copy below remains a proposal.

## The message hierarchy

1. **Brand idea:** Catalyst of analysis.
2. **Human tension:** the paralysis of analysis; more discussion without a clearer next move.
3. **Value:** turn thoughtful analysis into considered forward motion.
4. **Mechanism:** a clear question, independent perspectives, explicit conditions, an accountable owner.
5. **Product line:** Ask separately. Decide clearly.

Use the exact phrase **Catalyst of analysis.** at a major brand moment. Do not rewrite it as “analysis catalyst,” “AI catalyst,” or “catalyst for everything.” Supporting sentences do the explaining. Do not put five slogans in a single field.

## Core copy

**One sentence**

DecisionInvitation turns a decision that keeps circulating into a clear question, independent perspectives, and a more useful next move.

**Short introduction**

When analysis starts going in circles, another conversation can add more noise. DecisionInvitation gives the question a clear shape, invites the perspectives that matter, and keeps their reasoning in view. Ask separately. Decide clearly.

**Expanded introduction**

Some decisions need more thought. Others need the thought to start moving somewhere. DecisionInvitation helps the owner frame one decision, invite people to respond independently, and review the reasoning and conditions behind their positions. The next move might be a choice, a changed assumption, or the one fact still missing. The owner makes the call.

These are positioning statements. Replace future-facing capability language with verified present behavior for a live product announcement.

## Message pillars

| Pillar | Human meaning | Example | Evidence needed for a product claim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Give analysis a purpose | A clear question reduces circular work | “What would change the decision?” | A real framing workflow or explicitly illustrative specimen |
| Preserve independent thought | People can state a position before seeing others' answers | “Ask for the reasoning before the room converges.” | Verified response visibility and admission rules |
| Make conditions useful | A qualified answer can reveal the next move | “The answer is yes, if the delivery date holds.” | A real conditional response and its authorized owner view |
| Keep the call accountable | The owner can explain the decision | “A perspective informs the call. The owner makes it.” | Verified attribution and decision record for feature-level claims |

## Audience adaptation

For the owner, lead with the stuck question and what would move it forward. For the invitee, lead with why their perspective matters, what they are being asked to provide, and who will see it. For an organizational buyer, explain the workflow and operating boundary before any ambition. For a designer or partner, explain the identity's consistency and its exact limits.

## Voice

| Trait | Behavior | Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Purposeful | Connect thought to a useful next step | “Which condition would change your view?” | “Let's explore the endless possibilities.” |
| Exact | State the question, deadline, and audience | “Reply by 18 September, 17:00 Phoenix time.” | “Respond soon.” |
| Open | Make disagreement and missing evidence legitimate | “Name the fact you still need.” | “Help us confirm the right answer.” |
| Accountable | Distinguish advice, inference, and decision | “Morgan will review your response and make the decision.” | “The platform finds the best answer.” |

Be composed without becoming ceremonial. Use direct second-person instructions where helpful. Prefer “response,” “reason,” “condition,” “question,” and “next step” to specialized decision-science language. “Perspective” means the particular view someone was invited to bring; define it through context.

## Tone by situation

| Situation | Tone | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public brand statement | Confident and spacious | “Catalyst of analysis.” then a plain-language explanation |
| Invitation | Specific and respectful | “[Owner] is asking for your perspective on [question].” |
| Reminder | Brief and factual | “Your response is due [date, time, timezone].” |
| Missing information | Constructive | “Name what you need to take a position.” |
| Submission success | Precise | “Your response was recorded at [time].” only after a durable receipt |
| Failure | Calm and useful | “Your response was not saved. Try again, or contact [verified support route].” |
| Closed invitation | Definite | “This invitation closed at [time]. New responses are no longer accepted.” |

Dates must include a timezone when the deadline matters. Do not use “yesterday” or “soon” where later reading could change the meaning.

## Privacy microcopy

Use a deployment-specific statement with the actual permitted audience. Example pattern: “Your response is visible to [authorized audience]. Other invitees cannot view it.” Do not publish the pattern until the visibility rule has been verified for that deployment.

Do not say “anonymous,” “only you,” “completely confidential,” or “end-to-end encrypted” merely because responses are separate. A private perspective can still be attributed to an authorized owner.

## Editorial conventions

- Sentence case for headings, navigation, buttons, and captions.
- Preserve `DecisionInvitation`; no `Decision Invitation`, `Decision Invite`, or `DecisionInvitation AI` as a brand substitute.
- Use “Catalyst of analysis.” as the primary brand line. “Ask separately. Decide clearly.” is supporting language.
- Make statements at the strength of their evidence. “Designed to help” describes intent; it does not establish a measured outcome.
- Avoid guaranteed results, speed claims, synthetic consensus, urgency theater, and unsupported superlatives.
- Use ordinary text in subject lines, URLs, alt text, and metadata. The monogram does not change spelling or pronunciation.
