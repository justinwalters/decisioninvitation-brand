# Brand architecture and partnerships

## Relationship

DecisionInvitation is conceived as a standalone product within the aSUKIra family. It should be recognizable without the parent, useful without Suki, and coherent with the parent's care for identity. The user described an acquired-product feeling as creative inspiration. That is not evidence of a transaction, date, legal structure, or corporate history.

| Layer | DecisionInvitation rule |
| --- | --- |
| Public product identity | DecisionInvitation name, Di mark, own palette/type/composition |
| Subtle lineage | Signal Orange point in the monogram |
| Optional corporate attribution | A quiet, factual footer line when context warrants |
| Optional Suki connection | A separate, accurately described capability, never the product's definition |
| Legal/operator identity | Supplied and verified legal wording, separate from creative brand architecture |

`A product in the aSUKIra family` is a proposed editorial attribution based on the user's stated relationship. Before use in legal, contractual, procurement, or security contexts, the actual operator and entity wording must be confirmed. Do not use “acquired by,” “a subsidiary of,” “powered by,” or “a Suki company” as interchangeable phrases. They mean different things.

## Placement hierarchy

Lead with DecisionInvitation on its homepage, application, invitations, documents, and profile avatar. Corporate attribution may appear in the website footer, a press boilerplate, or an about page. It does not sit in the primary signature, every header, favicon, or primary CTA. No parent badge is necessary to authenticate the product.

A Suki integration may use the plain name in the capability description or a separately documented integration component. Explain its actual role and availability. Do not place a Suki icon beside every decision or imply autonomous execution. A disconnected experience must remain linguistically complete.

## Co-branding

Establish who owns the communication. A DecisionInvitation-led application uses this system and gives a partner a secondary attribution area. A partner-led application uses the partner's system and the supplied DecisionInvitation signature. A genuinely joint communication can use equal optical prominence, but never splice marks or make a new combined symbol.

Maintain each logo's clear space. Separate identities by at least the larger required clear-space measure; use a neutral gap or hairline outside both areas. Match apparent visual weight rather than forcing identical widths. Use one-color variants if color combinations conflict. Do not recolor a partner mark to fit this palette without permission.

Record partner permission, owner, exact wording, surface, duration, final proof, and withdrawal path. A customer logo in a product mockup is not a permitted endorsement. Testimonials, certification marks, badges, sponsor relationships, and reseller claims require their own verification. Archive expired partner compositions outside current public downloads.
