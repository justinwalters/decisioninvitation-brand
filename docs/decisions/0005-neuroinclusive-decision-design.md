# ADR 0005: Neuroinclusive decision design

- **Status:** Proposed with identity 0.4.0; product adoption and outcome claims remain unverified
- **Date:** 2026-09-14

## Context

DecisionInvitation reduces a socially noisy decision into a bounded question, independent perspectives, explicit conditions, and an owner-held next move. That structure may be particularly useful when working memory, task initiation, interruption recovery, rapid social processing, or ambiguous instructions create avoidable load. Neurodivergent experience is heterogeneous, and adjacent research does not prove a product-specific clinical or performance outcome.

## Decision

Position DecisionInvitation as **neuroinclusive by design**, not as a treatment or diagnosis-specific tool. Use the line **“Designed for minds that do their best thinking with context, time, and a clear next step.”** as the primary neuroinclusive expression.

Require product applications to orient before asking, externalize context, separate cognitive stages, preserve unfinished work, normalize conditional answers, disclose visibility, support assistive technology, and return the final decision to a named owner. Keep these requirements distinct from claims that the current deployed product has implemented or validated them.

## Consequences

- The existing verbal platform remains unchanged.
- The public brand center can explain the design intent and evidence boundary.
- Product handoffs must include neuroinclusive interaction requirements.
- “Treats executive dysfunction,” “eliminates ADHD paralysis,” “clinically proven,” and equivalent outcome claims are prohibited without direct, appropriate evidence.
- “Neuroinclusive by design” should not be used as a conformance badge until implementation and participatory testing support it.
- The orange provenance point remains non-semantic and must not signal urgency, attention, or completion.

## Validation

Conduct paid participatory research with neurodivergent adults and a broader comparison sample. Measure comprehension, completion, return after interruption, clarification requests, representation of dissent and conditions, perceived load, time to a recorded next move, and accessibility failures. Publish limitations and do not generalize preference findings into clinical claims.
