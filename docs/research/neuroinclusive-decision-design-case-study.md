# DecisionInvitation: a neuroinclusive decision instrument

**Deep case study and competitive landscape**  
**Version 0.4.0 proposal · 14 September 2026**

## Executive finding

DecisionInvitation has the potential to own a useful category boundary: not another task manager, survey, meeting tool, or consensus platform, but a **decision instrument** that helps a responsible owner turn a cognitively and socially noisy question into independent perspectives, explicit conditions, and a clear next move.

Its strongest neurodivergence story is structural, not clinical. It externalizes context that might otherwise have to be held in working memory; separates framing, responding, synthesis, and commitment; makes asynchronous written contribution legitimate; and names who decides and what happens next. These choices align with established cognitive-accessibility guidance and common accommodations for attention, memory, communication, and task initiation.[1–6] They may reduce avoidable cognitive and social load. They do not establish that the product treats executive dysfunction or improves clinical outcomes.

The strategic line is:

> **Designed for minds that do their best thinking with context, time, and a clear next step.**

The inclusive promise beneath it is:

> **Neuroinclusive by design. Useful for anyone deciding under cognitive load.**

This strengthens rather than replaces the existing platform: **Catalyst of analysis. Better input. Clearer decisions. Ask separately. Decide clearly.**

## 1. The human problem: analysis becomes an executive-function load

A consequential decision is rarely one mental act. It asks people to understand an ambiguous question, retain context, compare options, predict consequences, inhibit premature responses, shift perspective, communicate a position, remember a deadline, and initiate the next action. Those demands overlap with executive-function domains commonly studied in ADHD and autism, although neurodivergent profiles are heterogeneous and no single deficit model describes every person.[1–4]

Meta-analytic evidence in adults with ADHD reports group-level differences across working memory, inhibition, set shifting, and planning, while also warning that executive-function measures are neither universal nor specific enough to define every individual.[1][2] Reviews of autistic adults likewise report a mixed profile: group-level difficulty in areas such as flexibility and working memory can coexist with strengths in planning or decision-making, with considerable variation among people and tasks.[3][4]

The design implication is not “neurodivergent people cannot decide.” It is the opposite: **do not make good judgment depend on memory, rapid turn-taking, tolerance for interruption, or comfort performing certainty in a room.**

Decision paralysis also cannot be reduced to “too many choices.” A meta-analysis of choice overload found that negative effects depend on conditions such as choice-set complexity, task difficulty, preference uncertainty, and the decision goal.[7] The opportunity is therefore not to remove thought. It is to give thought a threshold and a route forward.

## 2. The mechanism: from cognitive load to decision movement

DecisionInvitation's product thesis can be expressed as five linked design moves.

### 2.1 Externalize the question

A well-framed invitation puts the decision, relevant context, criteria, owner, visibility, and deadline in the environment rather than asking a participant to reconstruct them. Research on cognitive offloading describes how external actions such as notes and reminders can reduce internal memory demand, while also cautioning that offloaded information must remain available and trustworthy.[5]

**Product requirement:** one bounded question per invitation; a preview of what is being asked, why the recipient was invited, who can see the response, how long it should take, and when it closes.

### 2.2 Separate thinking from social performance

Live groups introduce turn-taking, interruption, status, and evaluation pressures. Electronic brainstorming research has long examined how parallel contribution can reduce production blocking, although anonymity and outcome effects vary by setting.[8] Delphi-style research similarly uses staged, often anonymous input to reduce domination and support independent judgment, but DecisionInvitation should not claim to be Delphi and must not call private responses anonymous unless identity handling actually supports that mode.[9]

**Product requirement:** participants respond independently before cross-participant influence can reshape their answer. The owner sees attribution according to the disclosed authorization model.

### 2.3 Make qualified answers first-class

Binary responses often hide the information that moves a decision: “yes, if,” “no, unless,” “not yet,” and “I need this fact.” A structured response should preserve position, reason, condition, and missing evidence rather than flattening them into a vote.

**Product requirement:** support explicit response states—yes, no, not yet, yes if, no unless, and need information—with optional structured prompts for reason, condition, risk, and missing fact.

### 2.4 Make progress resumable

Cognitive accessibility guidance recommends clear steps, understandable content, support for personalization, and interfaces that help a person resume after distraction.[10][11] Accommodations commonly suggested for ADHD and autism include written instructions, checklists, reminders, task separation, advance questions, and electronic information sharing.[12–14]

**Product requirement:** autosave with a durable receipt, save-and-return, a visible step indicator, an explicit exit path, controlled reminders, keyboard and screen-reader support, 200 percent zoom, reduced motion, and no meaning carried by color alone.

### 2.5 Close the loop without stealing responsibility

The product should end in an owner-held decision, decision condition, or named missing fact—then expose the next action. Research on implementation intentions suggests that specific if-then plans can improve prospective-memory performance in some contexts, but effects vary and should not be generalized into an efficacy claim for this product.[15–17]

**Product requirement:** the owner records the decision or threshold, a concise rationale, the next action, and the person responsible. A synthesis is evidence for the owner, not objective truth or automatic consensus.

## 3. Neuroinclusive experience principles

1. **Diagnosis is never the price of access.** Helpful structure is available to everyone.
2. **Orient before asking.** Show the task, audience, time expectation, visibility, and deadline up front.
3. **One cognitive job at a time.** Separate understanding, responding, reviewing, and committing.
4. **Preserve unfinished thought.** Autosave, show state, and make re-entry obvious.
5. **Allow depth without requiring it.** Offer concise and detailed paths without hiding essential information.
6. **Normalize conditionality.** “I need more information” is useful decision data, not failure.
7. **Remove shame from reminders.** Let people control channel, cadence, snooze, and quiet hours where implemented.
8. **Keep language literal and calm.** Avoid urgency theater, idioms in instructions, and praise that obscures state.
9. **Disclose social boundaries.** Say who can see a response and whether other contributors can see it.
10. **Return agency to the owner.** The system clarifies; the accountable person decides.

## 4. Why the product can be exceptional

Most adjacent products optimize one part of the problem. Planners help an individual organize work. Scheduling systems decide when work fits. Forms collect answers. Polls tally preferences. Workshop canvases help a group think together. Governance platforms help groups deliberate and reach collective outcomes.

DecisionInvitation can connect four elements that are usually split across those categories:

- **Independent input:** contributors are not required to perform their thinking in a meeting or shared thread.
- **Decision-grade structure:** a response includes reasoning, conditions, and missing information—not merely a score or vote.
- **Explicit authority:** the product says who owns the call instead of laundering a decision through “the group.”
- **Forward condition:** success can be a decision, a threshold, or the next fact required—not forced certainty.

That combination turns “Catalyst of analysis” into a product behavior. Analysis is respected, bounded, and connected to action.

## 5. Competitive landscape

The relevant market is a set of neighboring categories rather than one direct substitute.

| Product/category | Primary job | Neuroinclusive strength | Where DecisionInvitation can differ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Loomio** | Group discussion, polls, proposals, and collective governance | Asynchronous participation, reasoning, deadlines, reminders, blind or anonymous voting options | Loomio centers group deliberation and collective outcomes. DecisionInvitation can center independent advice to a named decision owner.[18–21] |
| **GroupMap** | Facilitated brainstorming, workshops, voting, and consensus | Individual contribution modes, anonymity controls, show/hide results, structured facilitation | Strong group-work surface; DecisionInvitation can be smaller, calmer, and designed around a single accountable decision rather than a workshop.[22][23] |
| **Miro Private Mode** | Temporarily hide collaborators' content during a board activity | Reduces immediate group influence inside a familiar collaboration space | A facilitation feature, not a complete decision protocol; Miro also states that Private Mode is not a data-protection feature.[24] |
| **Polly** | Polls, surveys, standups, and employee feedback in Slack/Teams | Asynchronous participation inside existing work channels | Optimized for polling and engagement workflows. DecisionInvitation can preserve richer reasoning and decision ownership.[25][26] |
| **Typeform** | Flexible forms, surveys, logic, reports, and one-question-at-a-time collection | Conversational pacing and conditional logic can reduce form density | A general collection engine; the customer must invent the decision protocol, ownership model, and closure step.[27–29] |
| **Tiimo** | Visual planning and daily structure for people including ADHD/autistic users | Visual timelines, routines, focus support, flexible planning, AI checklists | Strong individual execution support; not built to gather independent perspectives for an owner's consequential decision.[30] |
| **Goblin Tools** | Small tools for task breakdown, estimation, rewriting, and decision support | Explicitly reduces complexity; one-task-at-a-time and braindump-to-actions patterns | Excellent individual utilities; DecisionInvitation can own the multi-person handoff from perspective to accountable decision.[31–34] |
| **Sunsama / Motion** | Guided daily planning, timeboxing, workload management, and auto-scheduling | Externalizes plans and reduces scheduling overhead | These help execute chosen work. DecisionInvitation operates earlier, where the question and conditions for action are still unsettled.[35–37] |
| **Slido** | Live and advance audience Q&A, polls, and meeting engagement | Anonymous question options can lower participation barriers | Event-centered and audience-facing; not a persistent owner-led decision record.[38] |

### Competitive white space

The defensible wedge lies between **neuroinclusive personal planning** and **collaborative decision governance**:

> A calm, asynchronous protocol for collecting independent judgment and converting it into an accountable next move.

The closest strategic competitor is Loomio, because it combines asynchronous discussion, reasoning, deadlines, and outcomes. DecisionInvitation must therefore avoid becoming a smaller poll tool. Its differentiation should be visible in the workflow: private-by-disclosure independent perspectives, structured conditions, an owner dashboard that preserves dissent, and a final decision/next-step record.

## 6. Brand and product expression

### Public positioning

Use:

- **Designed for minds that do their best thinking with context, time, and a clear next step.**
- **Neuroinclusive by design. Useful for anyone deciding under cognitive load.**
- **Different minds need different conditions for contributing.**
- **Make room for thinking. Make the next move visible.**

Do not use:

- “Treats executive dysfunction.”
- “Built for every neurodivergent brain.”
- “Clinically proven to improve decisions.”
- “Eliminates ADHD paralysis.”
- “Bias-free,” “confidential,” or “anonymous” without verified definitions and evidence.

### Interaction language

- “Here is what you are being asked to consider.”
- “Your response is saved. You can return before the invitation closes.”
- “Name the condition that would change your view.”
- “Not enough information is a useful answer. What is missing?”
- “The owner will review each perspective and make the decision.”

### Visual behavior

The Multipath D already encodes the idea: distinct inputs remain legible while moving toward a decision point. Neuroinclusive application should reinforce that metaphor through linear progress, strong hierarchy, limited simultaneous choices, persistent state, restrained motion, and no decorative competition with the task. The Suki-orange dot remains provenance—not urgency, status, or an attention alarm.

## 7. Evidence and claims boundary

No direct study currently establishes that DecisionInvitation improves executive function, reduces ADHD or autistic distress, increases decision quality, or shortens time to decision. The evidence supports the relevance of its design mechanisms, not the efficacy of this specific product.

Permitted language:

- “Designed to reduce avoidable cognitive and social load.”
- “Supports asynchronous, structured contribution.” when implemented and verified.
- “Neuroinclusive by design.” when the documented accessibility behaviors are implemented and tested with neurodivergent participants.

Stronger outcome claims require product-specific research. Accessibility conformance, privacy, anonymity, delivery, retention, and security claims require their own evidence.

## 8. Validation program

The next phase should include paid, participatory research with diagnosed and self-identified ADHD and autistic adults, alongside a broader comparison group. Neurodivergent people should shape the protocol, language, and interpretation—not merely test a finished interface.

Compare a structured DecisionInvitation flow with a shared thread, live meeting, or general form. Measure:

- invitation comprehension without clarification;
- time to first meaningful response;
- completion and return-after-interruption rates;
- unsubmitted or lost work;
- number of clarification requests;
- contributor confidence that their reasoning was represented;
- owner ability to identify dissent, conditions, and missing facts;
- time from invitation to a recorded next move;
- perceived cognitive and social load;
- keyboard, screen-reader, zoom, focus, and reduced-motion failures.

Use interviews to understand *why* a measure changed. Report subgroup sizes, limitations, attrition, and adverse experiences. Do not convert preference data into a clinical claim.

## 9. Strategic conclusion

DecisionInvitation is compelling because it does not ask people to think less. It creates better conditions for thought to become usable.

For neurodivergent participants, that can mean less reliance on memory, less pressure to answer in real time, fewer ambiguous social rules, and a safer place for conditional or incomplete answers. For decision owners, it means clearer evidence without pretending the software owns the consequence. For organizations, it creates a repeatable boundary between advice and authority.

That is the product's category opportunity:

> **DecisionInvitation is the decision instrument for independent thought, visible conditions, and accountable motion.**
