# Email, documents, and physical identity

Correspondence should feel considered, precise, and recognizable with almost no decoration. The product name and clear structure do more work than a large logo.

## Email

Use a text-led layout, one primary action, and a readable fallback. The signature contains a small full lockup or the plain product name, role/contact fields supplied by the sender, and the verified domain. Do not invent a mailbox, postal address, registered entity, office, or telephone number. Keep all template placeholders visibly bracketed until supplied.

Invite copy answers four questions: what is being decided, who can view my response, what contribution is requested, and when the invitation closes. The exact privacy statement must match the deployed access model. Deadline, reminder, delivery, and access labels must be backed by current state. Never promise anonymity by calling an attributed owner-only response private.

Default email structure: descriptive subject; direct opening; question and short context; one action; deadline if real; access/privacy explanation; sender identity; required operational footer. Do not embed raw invitation tokens in public mockups. Do not add tracking pixels or remote fonts as a brand requirement. A clickable signature image has meaningful alternative text; decorative imagery is optional.

## Stationery and business cards

Working formats are A4 (210 × 297 mm) and US Letter (8.5 × 11 in) for correspondence, and 3.5 × 2 in for a card. These are layout formats, not printer-ready certification. Keep a 15–20 mm page margin on correspondence. Let the signature occupy a quiet top-left area, with the main content on a consistent reading axis and a restrained footer.

Business card: mark/full name on one face; supplied person, role, verified contact, and domain on the other. Use only information that is safe and authorized to publish. QR codes require a verified destination, sufficient quiet zone, and physical scan testing; the identity package does not require one.

Print proofs must use the intended stock, printer profile, trim, bleed, and finishing specifications. The supplied RGB assets are not a substitute for vendor preflight. Do not label exported PDFs “press-ready,” “CMYK approved,” or “Pantone matched” without that work. Material selection is a creative recommendation, not a sustainability claim.

## Reports and presentations

Use a typographic cover with one thought, one signature, and a visible decision point. Inside, one claim per section and an explicit source or caption for evidence. Use Sora Regular for reading and Sora SemiBold for page labels, dates, identifiers, and decisions. Distinguish analytical inputs from the final accountable choice through input paths and rules, not a second typeface.

A report distinguishes question, evidence, perspective, proposed next step, and owner decision. A presentation must not flatten uncertainty into a decorative consensus score. Tables align comparable units; charts directly label series and expose sample/evidence limitations. Color alone never defines a contributor or state.

Every external document includes product name, date/version when material, and proposal/approved status when relevant. Internal governance and customer-sensitive evidence do not belong in a public press kit. Accessibility and export checks include selectable text, readable contrast, logical order, working links, and a rendered page-by-page inspection.
