# Claims, privacy, and evidence boundaries

This is an internal content-control guide, not legal advice or a security assessment. Brand presentation must never exceed what the product and release evidence establish.

## Source priority

The sibling `suki-decisioninvitation/PRODUCT.md` is the current product-positioning source. `MEDIATED-DECISION-DESIGN.md` records useful interaction and privacy principles but includes an older preview implementation and a different Core-authority framing. Do not merge those as if they describe one verified deployment. Escalate discrepancies to the product owner, and update implementation/product documentation separately from identity work.

## Claim register

| Wording/topic | Permitted handling | Evidence required for stronger claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Catalyst of analysis | Brand idea: thoughtful analysis can lead to a next move | Not a scientific, clinical, or performance guarantee |
| Ask separately. Decide clearly. | Supporting product promise | Specific workflow statements still need implementation evidence |
| Standalone product | Current intended product position | Live acceptance for claims of current public availability |
| Private contributions | Explain exact access: contributor and authorized owner as implemented | Deployed role/access tests and participant disclosure |
| Anonymous responses | Do not use as a synonym for private | Verified identity handling and an intentional anonymous mode |
| Secure / encrypted / end-to-end encrypted | Do not add as generic reassurance | Specific architecture, scope, implementation, testing, and approved wording |
| Invitations delivered | Show only when delivery receipts establish it | Actual channel/provider receipt, not draft creation |
| Persistent / retained / deleted | Match the deployed data behavior | Storage/retention/deletion behavior and approved policy |
| Suki-assisted synthesis/follow-up | Describe as optional and availability-dependent | Verified integration, authority, preview/approval, and execution receipts |
| Free / Pro / subscription | Packaging is not a brand decision | Approved current plan definitions and live billing acceptance |
| Compliant / certified / audited | No unsourced badges or shorthand | Exact scope, entity, date, assessor, and permission to publish |
| Customer outcome or speed | No invented metrics or testimonials | Consented, relevant, attributable evidence and limitations |
| Neuroinclusive by design | Use only for implemented and participatively tested structural choices; explain what they are | Current accessibility acceptance and paid neurodivergent participant research |
| Executive-function support | Describe the design mechanism, such as externalized context or resumable steps | Product-specific usability evidence for any measured outcome |
| Treats ADHD/autism or executive dysfunction | Do not use | Appropriate clinical evidence, regulatory/legal review, and explicit approval |

## Neurodivergence and research claims

Neurodivergent profiles are heterogeneous. Do not present ADHD, autism, dyslexia, or another identity as one fixed deficit profile. Do not imply that neurodivergent people are inherently indecisive. The product position is that a clear environment can remove avoidable demands on memory, rapid social processing, interruption recovery, and task initiation while preserving a person's judgment.

Adjacent research can support a mechanism hypothesis; it does not prove product efficacy. “Designed to reduce avoidable cognitive and social load” is an intent statement. “Improves executive function,” “reduces paralysis,” and outcome comparisons require direct, relevant product research. See `docs/research/neuroinclusive-decision-design-case-study.md`.

## Participant respect

Do not imply other participants can see a response, their identities, submission status, or attachments unless the actual authorization model allows it. Do not imply an owner cannot see attribution when the owner can. Explain the visibility of responses before asking someone to submit. “Private” without a clear audience can mislead.

Treat an invitation URL as a capability-bearing secret where applicable. Public specimens use obvious fictional labels and nonfunctional example paths. Never include a real token, private decision question, participant name, email, attachment, receipt, or customer screenshot in assets, tests, social previews, or a public repository.

An owner makes the call. Synthesis must not be described as objective truth, automatic consensus, or a binding decision. Reasons, dissent, conditions, and uncertainty should remain legible. A model-generated suggestion is not owner approval. A proposed follow-up is not an executed action.

## Release language

Separate “designed,” “built locally,” “included in an archive,” “uploaded,” “deployed,” and “verified live.” A local screenshot proves local rendering. A clean validator proves its checks passed. Neither proves privacy, access isolation, delivery, public availability, or the business's legal status.

When a claim is uncertain, narrow it, label it as proposed/planned, or omit it. Do not repair a gap in evidence with reassuring adjectives. Brand reviewers check copy; product/security/legal owners approve claims in their respective scopes. Record those approvals in the release brief, with no secrets in the public repository.
